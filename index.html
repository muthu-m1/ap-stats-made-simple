    The stats lab

    Ideas stick better when you can poke them. Drag, click and press the buttons to watch statistics happen.

    Units 1 and 2 · z-scores and the normal curve

    How much of the curve is shaded?

    A z-score says how many standard deviations (steps) you are from the middle. Slide it and watch the shaded part. The shaded area is the percent of values in that part.

    Unit 2 · central limit theorem

    Grab samples, graph their means

    The top picture is the whole population. Each sample grabs n values and finds their mean. The bottom graph collects those means. Watch it turn into a bell, even when the top isn't one.

    Unit 3 · confidence intervals

    How often does the net catch the fish?

    Pretend we know the truth: 60% of all students like pizza best. Each line is one sample's confidence interval. Purple lines catch the true 60%. Orange lines miss it. About 95% of 95% intervals should catch it.

    Unit 5 · regression

    Click to add dots, watch the line

    Click or tap the graph to add points. The line of best fit and the correlation r update each time. Try adding one far-away point and see how much it pulls the line.

    Which test do I use?

    Every inference question on the exam uses the same four steps: State, Plan, Do, Conclude. First ask what kind of data you have.

    • Yes/no answers?Percents: use z for one or two proportions.
    • Numbers you can average?Means: use t for one or two means.
    • Same people measured twice?Paired data: use t on the differences.
    • Two category variables?A two-way table: use a chi-square test.

    The AP exam

    How the exam works for the course that starts in Fall 2026. It's 3 hours long, and you can use a graphing calculator and the formula sheet in both sections.

    Section I · 90 minutes · 50% of your score

    42 multiple-choice questions

    Every unit shows up. About how much of this section comes from each one:

      Section II · 90 minutes · 50% of your score

      4 free-response questions

      Each one is worth 10 points and counts the same: 12.5% of your score.

      1. Question 1: Asking and collectingWrite a good investigative question and plan how to collect the data (Practices 1 and 2).
      2. Question 2: Analyzing and explainingMake graphs or calculations, then say what they mean (Practices 3 and 4).
      3. Question 3: InferenceA complete significance test or confidence interval, from State to Conclude.
      4. Question 4: Putting it togetherA multi-part question that mixes ideas from different units (Practices 2, 3 and 4).

      The four statistical practices

      The skills every question checks, and about how much of the multiple-choice section uses each one.

      • 1 · Formulate questionsAsk an investigative question that data can answer.5–10%
      • 2 · Collect dataChoose fair ways to collect data, and the right inference method, hypotheses and possible errors.20–30%
      • 3 · Analyze dataMake tables and graphs, and calculate statistics, probabilities and intervals.25–35%
      • 4 · Interpret resultsExplain what results mean, check conditions, and back up claims in context.25–35%

      Source: College Board's AP Statistics Course and Exam Description, effective Fall 2026. Exam details can change, so check AP Central for the latest version.

          About, privacy and terms

          AP Stats, Made Simple has no accounts, ads or analytics. This is how it works and what it does with your information.

          How it's made

          The lessons, examples and practice questions were written with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, following the five units in College Board's AP Statistics Course and Exam Description (effective Fall 2026). The goal is plain words: if a 5th grader could follow the sentence, it stays.

          AI can make mistakes. Your textbook, your teacher and the official course description are the final word.

          The AI tutor

          When you ask Stat Buddy something, your question and the chat so far go to this site's small tutor server (a Cloudflare Worker). It asks Qwen3, a free open AI model, running on Cloudflare Workers AI, and sends the answer back. The tutor server doesn't save your questions. Each page visit gets a random number so the server can share questions fairly between students; it isn't stored and doesn't identify you. If you use your own Claude API key instead, your browser talks to Anthropic directly.

          Cloudflare handles those messages under its own privacy policy, and Anthropic does under its policy if you use your own key. Don't type private things like your full name, address or passwords into the chat.

          Privacy

          • The site collects no personal information. There are no accounts, trackers or analytics.
          • It saves your practice answers and score in your own browser so they're still there next time. They never leave your device.
          • If you add your own API key, it's kept only for this visit, unless you tick "Remember the key on this device."
          • The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security; see GitHub's privacy statement.
